The Problem It Solves
Reactive → Preventive Surveillance System
Current epidemiological surveillance systems issue reactive alerts: case counts arrive with a 2–3-week delay, when transmission is already widely established.

The 2024 outbreak in Argentina exceeded 582,000 confirmed cases — ten times the previous historical maximum — revealing that the SNVS lacks short-term predictive tools.

This project builds a predictive early-warning system using exclusively open-access public data: SNVS 2.0, NASA POWER, and the 2022 Census. The model estimates next-week cases using data available up to t−2, enabling public health teams to act before the peak.
Public Health Early Warning Open Data Argentina Dengue
Project Objective
To develop and compare dengue prediction models at departmental resolution in Argentina, capable of anticipating outbreaks using open-access epidemiological, climatic, and sociodemographic data, with statistically robust metrics and validation without temporal data leakage.

Feature Engineering
Feature Groups
🦠 Epidemiological lags
casos_lag1 … lag6, 4-week moving average, cumulative sum, recent maximum. Dominate the model (top 5 SHAP)
🌡️ Lagged climate variables
mean/max/min temperature, relative humidity, precipitation — with 2, 4 and 6-week lags biologically motivated.
📅 Cyclic seasonality
Sine/cosine encodings for epidemiological week and month (avoids week 52→1 discontinuities).
🦟 Derived biological indices
Accumulated growing degree days, vapour pressure deficit, optimal vector condition index (25–30°C + RH >60%).
Feature Selection
Pearson correlation + mutual information + Random Forest importance.
Strict temporal cutoff: computed only on 2018–2019 to prevent data leakage. The top 30 features are kept fixed across all walk-forward folds.

Why Is MAPE >100% Valid?
The 2024 outbreak was 10× the historical maximum. No model trained on 2018–2023 could anticipate that magnitude. MAPE measures relative error, and large relative errors are unavoidable for out-of-distribution events. What matters is the relative improvement over the baseline, which was statistically significant in 5 of 6 years (non-overlapping 95% CIs).
Project Decisions and Insights
Insight 1
The 2024 Outbreak Was an Out-of-Distribution Event
The 2024 outbreak was 10× the historical maximum. No model trained on 2018–2023 could anticipate that magnitude. This does not invalidate the model: the relative improvement over the baseline remains statistically significant (non-overlapping 95% CIs in 5 of 6 walk-forward years). The tool remains operationally useful.
Insight 2
Epidemiological Lags Dominate the Prediction
The 5 most important variables (SHAP) are case lags, not climate variables. Recent cases directly capture community transmission state — fumigation coverage, density of susceptibles, circulating serotypes — which environmental variables cannot capture with the same specificity.
Insight 3
EpiGNN: Spatial Graph Adds Value in Outbreaks, Not in Endemic Years
EpiGNN achieves MAE 14.3 in 2024 (best absolute) but MAPE ~99% in low-burden years. Conclusion: the spatial signal adds value when there are pronounced epidemic gradients between neighbouring departments. In low-transmission years, model complexity is not justified.
Insight 4
Data Leakage: The Most Dangerous Error in Epidemiological ML
Feature selection must be done using only training data (2018–2019). If 2024 is included, the model "chooses" variables that already know the outbreak, artificially inflating metrics by ~30%. This issue was detected and corrected during development — a concrete methodological contribution of this work.
Insight 5
Walk-forward Exposes Real Performance
Random cross-validation would have produced metrics ~30% better, but illusory ones. Walk-forward validation exposes performance under real operational conditions. This design decision is what makes the work publishable in a public health journal and deployable in the SNVS.
Insight 6
Temperature with 2-Week Lag Confirms the Biological Cycle
SHAP identifies temp_media_lag2 as the most relevant climate predictor. Extrinsic incubation period in Aedes aegypti (8–14 days) + intrinsic period in humans (3–14 days) = 14–28 days = 2–4-week lag. The model is capturing real biology, not spurious correlations.

What We Learned from the Process
🔧 Technical — What Nobody Teaches in Class
Data Integration > Modelling
Integrating 5 heterogeneous sources (SNVS with 2 different annual schemas, NASA POWER, Census, RENABAP, SMN) took longer than training all models combined. Data quality is the determining factor.
Temporal Data Leakage Is Subtle
In epidemiological ML, leakage does not come from mixing train/test — it comes from computing features using future data. Feature selection with 2024 included would inflate metrics by ~30%. We detected it late and corrected it.
High MAPE Is Not Failure
A MAPE of 101% in an outbreak 10× the historical maximum is expected and operationally valid if the relative improvement over the baseline is statistically significant. Metrics must be interpreted in context.
EpiGNN: Complexity ≠ Superiority
LightGBM outperformed EpiGNN in 5 of 6 walk-forward years despite being a simpler model. Complexity must be justified by statistically significant improvement, not just numerical gain in one year.
🧠 Methodological — Decisions That Make the Difference
Walk-forward Validation ≠ Random CV
Random CV would have given metrics ~30% better but illusory. Walk-forward simulates real operational conditions. That decision turns the work into something publishable and deployable.
Bootstrap CIs: A Point Estimate Is Not Enough
Reportar solo el MAPE puntual no informa si la diferencia es estadística o ruido. Los 95% Bootstrap CI permiten afirmar que LightGBM superó al baseline de forma robusta.
The Real Horizon Is 0–1 Weeks
Lags consume anticipation: in week i, the model uses data up to i−2 and predicts i+1. The real operational lead time is 0–1 week, not 4. Acknowledging this publicly is an honest methodological contribution.
SHAP Confirms Biology
That temp_media_lag2 is the key climate predictor, with a lag exactly within the range of the Aedes extrinsic cycle (8–14 days), is an internal model validation: it captures mechanism, not just correlation.
Team Reflection
"This project taught us that in machine learning applied to public health, methodological transparency is worth more than perfect metrics. A model that is honest about its limitations is infinitely more useful than one that overestimates its performance." — on correcting the real horizon and data leakage
"Building EpiGNN's graph with 520 departments and seeing it improve MAE tenfold in the largest outbreak in Argentine history was the most rewarding moment of the project. It was seeing that graph mathematics was capturing something real about how an epidemic spreads." — on EpiGNN and the 2024 outbreak
"Our biggest takeaway: before training the first model, we spent weeks normalising SNVS schemas, unifying department names across sources, and cleaning impossible values. Data is never clean. That is not taught in courses." — on the data integration pipeline
